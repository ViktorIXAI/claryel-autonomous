# P2005 load report for 2026-11-18 (full day, CET)

Verdict: **light-to-moderate**. The box was a resident inference server that was asked for
work in bursts; the busiest quarter of the day was 21:00-22:59, the second busiest was the
01:00-05:59 window. Nothing saturated: CPU never exceeded 31 % busy on its 32 logical CPUs,
GPU never exceeded 176 W, temperatures stayed under limit, swap stayed idle.

## Energy

* GPU: **724 Wh** on 2026-11-18 (integrating the 2-minute `gpu_w` channel; idle floor 8 W, mean 30 W, peak 176 W).
* GPU above 60 W (real inference load): **208 minutes** of 1440 (14 %). SM clock above 1000 MHz in 240 sample-minutes.
* CPU package: **49 Wh measured** over the 67 samples where RAPL existed (21:27-24:00); peak 58 W. Whole-day CPU energy is **estimated** at 300-450 Wh from that window plus the CPU-busy profile (mean 6 % busy) - it is not measured for 00:00-21:27.
* Total chip energy: roughly **1.0-1.2 kWh**, i.e. about **0.25-0.30 EUR/day** at 0.25 EUR/kWh. Wall energy would be 15-25 % higher but is not metered.
* Mains: `ac_online = 1` in all 67 power samples, battery power 0 W - no discharge, so no outage inside the measured window.

## What actually ran

* **Resident brain**: Basalt serve with the Qwen3.8-Flash IQ3_S model, resident 00:00-23:26, a gap 23:26-23:34, resident again until **23:58**, when it was stopped for the llama.cpp load campaign that continued into 19 Nov. GPU memory resident ~22-24 GiB, so no model reloads during the day (disk read peaks at night were 0.2-2.2 MB/s - metadata churn, not model loads).
* **LLM work served**: 1833 agent turns through the brain. Peaks: 02:00 (346), 21:00 (348), 22:00 (204), 05:00 (169), 04:00 (158), 15:00 (134), 03:00 (128), 23:00 (89), 17:00 (51), 09:00 (43).
* **Night window 01:00-05:59**: sustained serving (944 turns), GPU bursts to 169-176 W, CPU busy 3-26 %, load1 up to 21.
* **Day gap 07:00-14:59 and 16:00-20:59**: idle - GPU at the 8 W floor, CPU 0-1 % busy, load1 ~0.1.
* **21:00-22:59**: the heaviest hour of the day - GPU mean 130 W / peak 175 W, CPU 31 % busy, package 40-58 W, 348 LLM turns. This is also when the power/thermal telemetry itself was developed (thermal and CPU-cap scripts edited 21:46-21:53, RAPL columns appear at 21:27).
* **23:00-23:45**: compile and checkout campaign - Strata 0.2.16 CUDA build (23:16-23:20), IQ3_S smoke serve test (23:24-23:28, GPU burst 165 W at 23:22), llama.cpp clone (23:30-23:40, disk read spike 181 658 reads/s). Brain was then stopped at 23:58 for the load campaign.
* **Agent-driven maintenance**: 249 SSH logins from claryel01 during the day, concentrated at 23:00 (59) and 21:00 (41).
* **Local watchdogs**: `keep-up.sh` fired 714 times (~every 2 min), plus thermal/power/epp/fleet samplers - constant background, visible as run-queue 4-6 with CPU ~0 %.

## Parallelism

Measured from 5-minute buckets of sessions hitting the brain: 81 buckets had traffic,
**76 with exactly one session and 5 with two** - the brain never served more than two agent
sessions at once (25 minutes in total at two). On top of that the box always had 4-6
watchdog/sampler processes runnable. So the honest answer to "two, three, four tasks in
parallel": **two concurrent LLM clients maximum all day, and 3-4 distinct activities only in
23:16-23:45** (build + smoke test + clone + resident brain).

A load average of 21 does **not** mean 21 cores were busy: load counts runnable *and*
uninterruptible tasks, and inference serving parks most of its work in GPU waits. That is why
load1 reached 21 while CPU busy stayed at 31 % - roughly 10 of the 32 threads were actually
cycling at the busiest moment.
