## Cold/warm ladder — primary pass (13:29–13:33 UTC)

Runner `benchmarks/ladder/ladder_bench.py`: fresh conversation per cold row,
filler prompt of repeated 17-word sentences + one question, `max_tokens 128`,
`temperature 0`. Raw rows: `results/p3001-rtx3050-iq3s-ladder-2026-11-11.jsonl`.
"TTFT" is the engine's own prompt-read accounting for the request; this serve
emits the completion as one SSE chunk, so a client-side first-token stamp is not
meaningful here (see the streaming pass below).

| tag | prompt tok | reused | **TTFT (prompt read) s** | prefill tok/s | decode tok/s | decode s | wall s | drafts accepted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| cold-2k | 1,568 | 0 | 11.86 | 132.2 | 24.3 | 5.27 | 17.2 | 74/100 = 74.0 % |
| cold-8k | 6,168 | 1,024 | 42.59 | 120.8 | 28.0 | 4.57 | 47.2 | 74/105 = 70.5 % |
| cold-16k | 12,268 | 1,024 | 87.52 | 128.5 | 30.7 | 4.17 | 91.7 | 79/94 = 84.0 % |
| warm-8k-a | 6,168 | 6,163 | 0.20 | (99.9 % cached, ratio meaningless) | 28.0 | 4.57 | 4.8 | 75/106 = 70.8 % |
| warm-8k-b | 6,168 | 6,163 | 0.33 | (99.9 % cached) | 28.7 | 4.47 | 4.8 | 75/103 = 72.8 % |

Method note kept on purpose: "cold" means a fresh conversation, not a cold cache.
The engine reuses the common 1024-token filler prefix (prompt-cache tail/spread),
so the 8k/16k rows are `1024 reused + rest read`; both numbers are published.
Expert-tier accounting for these rows (same log extract): decode expert cache hit
rate 66.6 % on cold-2k, 72.8–74.3 % on the 8k/16k and warm rows, with 3.9–5.8 % of
routed experts read by the GPU over PCIe (or from another GPU); the tier line
reports `since the start RAM <n> blobs, files 0 blobs 1161.1 MB read`, i.e. the
file tier is out of the request path. Warm rows: the engine restored the
6,163/6,168-token conversation checkpoint in 60.2–66.3 ms and re-parked it in
192.4–260.3 ms (snapshot ≈450–462 MB), which is the whole warm TTFT.

## Streaming control pass (13:52–14:05 UTC)

Same prompts, `stream=true` + `stream_options.include_usage`, client-side
first-content-delta stamp (`ladder_ttft_stream.py`). Raw rows:
`results/p3001-rtx3050-iq3s-ladder-2026-11-11.streaming.jsonl`.

| tag | client first-delta s | wall s | prompt tok | engine prompt read | decode tok/s | drafts |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---|
| ttft-cold-2k | 7.929 | 7.9 | 1,572 | 1024 reused + 548 read in 3212 ms (170.6 tok/s) | 27.2 | 72/101 |
| ttft-cold-8k | (no delta parsed) | 47.7 | 6,173 | 1024 + 5149 read in 42989 ms (119.8) | 27.4 | 72/110 |
| ttft-cold-16k | (no delta parsed) | 92.3 | 12,274 | 1024 + 11250 read in 87746 ms (128.2) | 28.5 | 73/104 |
| ttft-warm-8k-a | (no delta parsed) | 4.6 | 6,173 | 6168 reused, 205 ms | 29.2 | 75/102 |
| ttft-warm-8k-b | (no delta parsed) | 4.7 | 6,173 | 6168 reused, 331 ms | 29.1 | 76/104 |

Finding: on this serve the whole completion arrives as a **single content delta**,
so the client's first-delta time equals the total wall time (7.9 s vs 7.9 s), not
the time to first token. The engine's prompt-read accounting (11.9 s / 42.6 s /
87.5 s) is the TTFT figure to quote for P3001. The cold-2k streaming row is also
faster in prefill (170.6 tok/s vs 132.2 in the primary pass) because it inherited
a warm page cache from the primary pass — the two passes are not interchangeable.

## Real text on the same config — 25.7 tok/s, not 59

`realtext_bench.py` sent a genuine engineering request (write `rate_limiter.py`
plus pytest tests; 158 prompt tokens, `max_tokens 512`, `temperature 0`) once at
14:0x UTC. The client record was lost to a runner bug (the reply arrived in a
non-`content` message field, the script then crashed), but the engine logged the
request, and the engine's accounting is the measurement of record:

```
strata serve: prompt 158 tokens = 0 reused + 158 read in 2341 ms (67.5 tok/s),
512 generated in 19960 ms (25.7 tok/s), drafts accepted 252 of 352
```

So **real-text decode on the S3 (RTX 3050) config = 25.7 tok/s** (draft
acceptance 71.6 %, prefill of a short prompt 67.5 tok/s). The profile churn of
CHG-003 began minutes later, so no further real-text row exists for S3-a.

## The 59 tok/s figure — real text, but on earlier configs

The 59 tok/s number is real and it is real text, but it was **not** measured on
the RTX 3050 state. It appears in P3001's own serve log in two earlier states:

| state | date | GPU | engine build | request rows (engine accounting) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | 2026-11-02, engine up from 19:35:02 | RTX 3060 12 GB | `engines-0.1.55-avx` (sm_86) | `1230 tok prompt → 139 gen 59.2 tok/s, drafts 102/108`; `1225 → 117 gen 59.3, 88/93`; `1213 → 114 gen 59.7, 85/90` |
| S2 | 2026-11-03, engine up from 15:55:16 | Tesla P100-PCIE-16GB | `engines-0.1.55-avx60` (sm_60) | `1681 (1674 reused) → 725 gen 58.9, 545/545`; `1714 → 512 gen 59.1, 382/387` |

Population view from the same log (rows with ≥256 generated tokens, i.e. real
generation rather than one-word answers):

| state | requests | real-generation rows | decode min/median/max tok/s | draft acceptance (median) | median generated |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|
| S1 (RTX 3060) | 334 | 87 | **23.4 / 78.5 / 98.0** | 98.5 % | 518 |
| S2 (Tesla P100) | 38 | 16 | 8.0 / 33.1 / 59.1 | 45.9–58.1 % | 700 |
| S3 (RTX 3050) | 11 | 1 | 25.7 (single row) | 71.6 % | 512 |

Reading: 59 tok/s sits at the **low** end of S1's real-traffic band and at the
**top** of S2's band. What separates the states is draft acceptance, not the
card: S1's fleet-task traffic was highly predictable (98.5 % of MTP drafts
accepted), which is what pushed decode to 78–98 tok/s; S2/S3 acceptance was
46–72 %. Quoting "59 tok/s" for P3001 without its state label is therefore wrong
in both directions.

## Speculative decoding: acceptance and the limits that cap decode

* Flags unchanged across states: `--spec 4 --spec-min-p 0.5 --mtp rt` (4-deep MTP
  drafter). Acceptance on the synthetic ladder (S3) is 70.5–84.0 %; on real fleet
  traffic in S1 it was 96.5–100 % per row; on real text in S3, 71.6 %.
* The synthetic filler ladder is the worst case for drafting: repeated sentences
  still leave enough surprise to reject ~30 % of drafts, so ladder decode
  (24.3–30.7 tok/s) understates real-task decode on any state.
* S3 decode ceiling is set by the GPU cache, not by RAM: the engine holds
  **46.53 GiB of experts page-locked in host RAM** and only **557 experts in the
  GPU cache**, and 3.9–5.8 % of routed experts have to be read over PCIe per
  request. The 8 GiB card (12 GiB in S1, 16 GiB in S2) is what limits the GPU
  expert cache; the RAM upgrade took the file tier out of the request path
  (engine line: `no file reads`).
* Short prompts stay the box's weak spot (same diagnosis as 2026-11-02): a
  158-token real prompt prefilled at 67.5 tok/s and a 1568-token one at
  132 tok/s, while 8k–16k prompts hold 120–171 tok/s. With `--prefill 2048`
  (S3) there is less chunk amortisation than S1's `--prefill 4096`.

## Why these numbers are not a GPU comparison

S1 → S2 → S3 changed **at least seven things at once**; the GPU is only one of
them, and the traffic mix changed with it:

| item | S1 (2026-11-02) | S2 (2026-11-03) | S3 (2026-11-11) |
|---|---|---|---|
| compute GPU | RTX 3060 12 GB | Tesla P100 16 GB | RTX 3050 8 GB |
| engine build | 0.1.55-avx, archs `[86]` | 0.1.55-avx60, archs `[60]` | 0.1.55 engine-avx, archs `[86]` |
| RAM | 4 × 4 GiB DDR3 ECC (15.5 GiB) at passport collection; 16 GiB resident tier + `--memory-limit-mib 19456` in the serve profile | 62 GiB class (log: MemAvailable ≈30 GiB with 19 GiB limit) | 8 × 8 GiB DDR3 ECC 1333 (62 GiB), no explicit memory cap |
| `--prefill` | 4096 | 4096 | 2048 |
| `--max-context` | 32768 | 32768 | 16384 |
| expert flags | `--resident-experts --resident-budget-gib 16 --vram-reserve-mib 960` | same | `--resident-experts` (auto budget), `--gpu 1` pinned |
| traffic | 334 real fleet-task requests (PARB v2.1 sweep) | mixed probes/sweeps | 11 benchmark requests, no fleet traffic |

So this file documents **what the current P3001 state does**, and the S1/S2
numbers stay in their own documents as S1/S2 numbers. Any claim of the form
"RTX 3050 is X % slower than RTX 3060 on this box" is **not supported** by these
rows and must not be made from them. A controlled re-run would need the same
engine build, same flags, same context, same prompt set and the same RAM state,
with only the card swapped — recorded as an open item in CHG-002.

## Infrastructure failure in the same window (published per fleet rule)

At 14:11:29 UTC the on-box serve profile `~/Strata/strata-iq3_s.json` was
replaced by a 1417-byte variant (sha256/16 `92ea653bb413210d`) whose argument
list is malformed: the `--kv` flag is missing in one place (leaving a bare
`int8` after `--max-context 16384`) and `--gpu` lost its value. Two engine starts
failed with exit 2 and `unknown argument: int8` — **14:11:38 and 14:15:02 UTC** —
and a direct probe during that window found nothing listening on 8080 (curl exit 7).
`keep-up.sh` (cron `*/5` + `@reboot`) restarted the serve at 14:17:27, 14:20:02,
14:25:03 and 14:25:52 as the file was rewritten twice more; the profile in force at
14:35 UTC is a 1430-byte variant, sha256/16 **`f09f2af548fc8566`**, and `/health`
answers 200 again with a single listener and `resident RAM: 46.47 GiB`.

Two consequences for this record. (a) No measurement was lost: every row above was
served by profile **S3-a** (`418cdef198c263f8`, kept as
`~/Strata/strata-iq3_s.json.bak-16k-20261111`) in the 13:27–14:10 UTC window, before
the churn. (b) **The live profile is no longer the measured one** — the 14:25 variant
has not been benchmarked, so a new P3001 arm has to pin the profile hash before its
numbers may be compared with the rows above. This audit changed nothing on the box;
full record in `hardware/changes/P3001.md` CHG-003.

  `results/p3001-rtx3050-iq3s-ladder-2026-11-11.engine-lines.txt` (accounting
  lines only, account paths replaced by `$HOME`).
* Raw JSONL rows are the bytes the runners wrote: primary pass sha256

  `logs/INDEX.md`).
